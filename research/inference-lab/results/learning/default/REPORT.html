Online rule learning and curiosity

1,560 runs; 374,400 teaching turns; 12 conditional reasoner checks. All final component checkpoints reproduced predictions and the next question.

This is a structured-world experiment. A supplied vocabulary and conjunction hypothesis family are its inductive biases. It does not demonstrate language acquisition, concept discovery, neural training, or human-like intrinsic motivation.

Measured findings

  • Stable worlds: full-history with informative questions averaged 97.7% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • Stable worlds: drift-reset with informative questions averaged 93.9% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • Stable worlds: window with informative questions averaged 96.8% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • Changing worlds: full-history with informative questions averaged 70.5% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • Changing worlds: drift-reset with informative questions averaged 87.1% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • Changing worlds: window with informative questions averaged 86.3% held-out balanced accuracy over the teaching trajectory, averaging the configured noise levels and seeds.
  • The informative drift-reset learner averaged 1.24 false change alarms per stable run. Adaptation has a measurable retention cost.

What was tested

Each world has 6 binary attributes. Hidden targets have one to three signed conditions; changing worlds switch rules after questions [80, 160]. Feedback flip rates are [0.0, 0.1, 0.2]; every learner assumes 10% noise. The clean target, actual flip events, and phase boundaries remain evaluator-only. Question and evaluation pools are disjoint random halves, resampled to contain both outcomes in each phase.

All variants receive the same label budget. Corresponding runs share world, split, and Bernoulli flip schedule. Repeated questions receive fresh noisy responses. Memory-only is an exact example lookup with Beta(1,1) smoothing; unseen examples remain at probability 0.5. Frozen keeps its original rule prior. Information gain uses the learner's posterior and includes random exploration.

Rule learning and adaptation

Accuracy AUC is mean post-update held-out balanced accuracy over all turns; final accuracy averages the last 20 turns. Brier scores assess latent clean outcomes (lower is better). Teacher log loss is scored before the answer is learned, against the noisy response. Those are different quantities.

Resources and reproduction

Experiment wall time 114.4s; process CPU time 111.6s; controller peak RSS 40.3 MiB. RSS covers the entire run; CPU time excludes Rust child processes. No energy measurement is available. Per-run timings include teaching, scoring, event storage, and occasional proof checks; learner and policy timings are separate in raw results.

Observations and checkpoints are in memory.sqlite; evaluator metrics are separate. Conditional proof explanations never certify learned hypotheses. All components are single-process local implementations; the language bridge is a later plugin.

Reproduce with python -m research_lab learning run --config config.json --output NEW_DIRECTORY.
