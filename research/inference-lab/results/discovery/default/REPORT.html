Growing explanations in three discovery worlds

2,970 task runs; 712,800 teaching responses; 66 Python/Rust proof audits. Every final checkpoint reproduced predictions and its next question.

The learner constructs small rule programs from supplied building blocks. It does not invent an unrestricted language, acquire natural language, or update neural weights. This is a controlled comparison of existing research ideas.

What the comparison establishes

  • On stable relay tasks, adaptive expansion improved mean trajectory balanced accuracy over the small reset learner by 21.2 percentage points. This supports the value of a richer explanation language in these cases.
  • Adding the concept library changed stable-relay accuracy by +0.05 percentage points (curriculum bootstrap interval -0.51 to +0.43). Definition creation and reuse therefore do not establish an accuracy advantage here.
  • The broad-upfront baseline had the highest mean trajectory accuracy in 6/6 primary family/change groups. Its rich language and immediate candidate admission are a stronger package than the tested delayed-growth strategy; this is not an exhaustive-search oracle comparison.
  • On changing relays, adaptive expansion with reuse averaged 50.4% trajectory accuracy and 50.0% final accuracy, versus 68.1% and 86.5% for broad-upfront. This is a substantial adaptation failure of the tested configuration. A 32-response confirmation block is long relative to an 80-response phase; the experiment does not isolate that delay from candidate search, recent-evidence fitting, or other causes.
  • Purely random controls produced 0 accepted growth events across 90 runs. Fresh-response testing and a complexity cost reduce opportunities to overfit but do not certify discoveries.

Helpers are mined from final most-probable source programs, which may be wrong. Compression preserves their meaning; later predictive evidence must establish usefulness. Source-program selection itself is a limitation to study.

Learning and recovery costs

Success requires ten consecutive responses at held-out balanced accuracy ≥0.90. The delay ends when success is confirmed. Unrecovered transitions are censored at the next change or task end. Medians cover successes only, so they must be read alongside the success counts. An alarm episode starts when the detector flag rises from false to true. The raw per-task 'alarms' counter instead counts responses with a positive flag, which can remain latched during confirmation or in the broad baseline. These are not counts of distinct real-world changes. Episodes in unchanged worlds can indicate noise or an inadequate model.

Measured findings

  • Stable mechanism: broad-upfront had the highest mean trajectory accuracy (78.3%) among the tested configurations. This is descriptive, not a guarantee of superiority.
  • Changing mechanism: broad-upfront had the highest mean trajectory accuracy (70.7%) among the tested configurations. This is descriptive, not a guarantee of superiority.
  • Stable pair: broad-upfront had the highest mean trajectory accuracy (97.1%) among the tested configurations. This is descriptive, not a guarantee of superiority.
  • Changing pair: broad-upfront had the highest mean trajectory accuracy (83.2%) among the tested configurations. This is descriptive, not a guarantee of superiority.
  • Stable relay: broad-upfront had the highest mean trajectory accuracy (92.8%) among the tested configurations. This is descriptive, not a guarantee of superiority.

Each curriculum contains one mechanism task, three pair tasks, and a relay task. Object names are random. The measurement schema is shared and versioned; new object names do not imply a new meaning for the measurements. Scenes and allowable tests are public; targets, clean labels, feedback noise, and change boundaries remain evaluator-owned. This is an API/data-flow boundary between trusted local components, not a security sandbox.

The broad-upfront condition is a bounded search baseline, not exhaustive optimal search over every richer program. Its direct candidate admission and the adaptive fresh-response gate differ; comparisons assess these complete strategies. Information-seeking questions are used unless random/progress is specified.

Noise levels [0.0, 0.1, 0.2]; seeds [0, 1, 2, 3, 4]. The learner assumes 10% noise. Changing curricula switch mechanisms after responses [80, 160]. New explanations face 32 fresh responses. Improvement must exceed added program complexity plus 2.303 log-score units, and must improve raw prediction as well. This margin is a heuristic acceptance rule, not a calibrated significance test.

Concepts and explanations

A helper is accepted only if it occurs at least three times across two tasks and reduces corpus description size after paying for its definition. That is a semantics-preserving shortcut relative to the source programs; it does not establish that those programs describe reality. Definitions are immutable and versioned. Later prediction performance tests whether the shortcut is useful. Concept counts alone are not a success metric.

Resources and reproducibility

Teaching/evaluation loop: 898.3s wall, 2358.2s Python CPU; peak process RSS 114.3 MiB. CPU/RSS exclude native proof child processes. Timings include storage, checkpoint verification, and evaluation; search context counters separately charge candidate predictions on public training scenes. No energy sensor is available.

CPU sums worker teaching loops; RSS is the largest individual worker/controller peak, not simultaneous aggregate RSS. 3 independent curriculum workers. Immutable syntax/type caches may be warm across tasks; these wall times are not isolated cold-kernel comparisons.

Research and limits

  • Learning MDL Logic Programs from Noisy Data: Noise-tolerant program search with an explicit complexity cost. Our bounded grammar and fresh-response gate are a separate implementation; no published performance result is transferred.
  • Top-Down Synthesis for Library Learning (Stitch): Mine repeated symbolic structure to construct a reusable library. Shared-body mining here is simpler than Stitch, and compression alone does not establish correctness or new expressive power.
  • Hypothesis Generation and Inductive Inference in Children and Language Models: Generate hypotheses during learning instead of only reweighting an initial set. Recent box-task study; neither a reproduction nor evidence that this learner models children.
  • Beyond Noisy-TVs: Noise-Robust Exploration Via Learning Progress Monitoring: Compare prequential learning progress with uncertainty-seeking questions. Our grouped loss-window policy is inspired by the idea, not the neural LPM method or its guarantees.
  • DiscoveryWorld: Separate task completion from discovering explanations and executing experiments. These smaller worlds are custom diagnostics, not DiscoveryWorld benchmark results.
  • Formal Concept Analysis: A possible future alternative proposer based on object-attribute closure. Deferred: exact observed implications are not reliable causal laws under noisy feedback.
  • ADVENT: LLM-Driven Automatic Predicate Invention for ILP: A future language-model proposer behind the same verification boundary. Deferred to keep pretrained knowledge outside this experiment; published poker results do not establish general concept discovery.
